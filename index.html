In-app chat · pre-booking pages · new users only

Two sets of booking screens were live at once — and they get asked different questions

Between these two windows Wiom rolled out a new set of booking screens. The original six pages and the new education screens ran side by side, so the same chat box was answering two different audiences. What they asked changed completely.

Periods  10–30 Aug vs 1–20 Sep 2026 New users  32,849  /  62,743 Messages  20,752 People  7,755

Fewer new users needed to ask for help — but that is mostly because there were nearly twice as many new users, not because the product got clearer.

First-time app opens went from 32,849 to 62,743, roughly doubling per day. Chat volume grew too — 429 to 587 messages a day — but not nearly as fast, so the rate per 1,000 new users fell from 106.3 to 67.9. Read that as a dilution effect first and a clarity win second.

The real change is what gets asked. The new education screens went from a third of pre-booking chat in August to nearly six in ten by mid-September, and they provoke product-capability questions the old flow almost never saw: speed, number of devices, and whether the net reaches the whole house.

ScopeNew users only. The chat records messages, not openings.

Who is counted, and against what

Pre-booking chat is only reachable from the booking flow, and the identity on these rows is a booking-service id, not a phone number — it does not join to the customer base. So this population is new users by construction: nobody already on Wiom appears here.

“j2” is a screen name, not the booking variant J2

The new pages are recorded as j2_edu1, j2_cost and so on. That prefix is a screen-set name and is unrelated to the booking variant J2, which was effectively retired over these windows (302 bookings in the August window, 6 in September). Customers seeing these screens sit across every live booking variant — I3, J3, J4N and J4R. This report compares screen sets; for the booking-variant cut see the install report.

The denominator is not the one the July report used

That report used app-analytics installs supplied by hand (48,118 / 49,800). Those figures were not available for these windows, so this report derives its denominator from the warehouse instead — first-ever app open per mobile, from FCT_CUSTOMER_APP_OPENS: 32,849 for 10–30 Aug and 62,743 for 1–20 Sep. It is internally consistent across both windows here, but it measures a different thing, so per-1,000 rates in this report cannot be compared to the June/July report.

The rolloutA rollout, not a cutover. Both flows served real users all the way through.

The new screens arrived in the middle of the comparison

The new education screens were not switched on overnight. They ramped from about 17 August, crossed over the old flow around 19–23 August, and by September were carrying most of the traffic — but the old pages never went away. Both were live on every single day of both windows.

new education screens all pre-booking messages

This matters for every number in this report. August is mostly the original pages; September is mostly the new screens. So an Aug→Sep movement is partly a calendar change and partly a product change, and the two cannot be fully separated. Where it matters, the sections below compare the flows directly instead.

Chip → free textDid the canned answer finish the job?

Roughly a quarter of chip taps are followed by typing

22.3% of chip taps in August and 24.0% in September were followed by the same person typing their own message. That is the clearest available signal that a canned answer did not land — and it got slightly worse, not better, as the new flow took over.

It is not a clean failure rate: some of those follow-ups are a second, different question rather than a restatement of the first. But the direction is worth watching, because the j2 chips are now answering harder questions than the old ones did.

What to fixOrdered by volume against difficulty, not by severity alone.

Where the communication still needs work

01

Put chips on j2 plans and j2 slot selection

Frustration 12.6% and 12.0% · chip share under 6%

These two pages make people type, and typing on a transactional page is where frustration concentrates. Every neighbouring page solves this with three or four chips.

Change: Ship the same chip pattern used on the education pages — the three or four questions actually asked here — before the rollout finishes and the volume arrives.
02

Answer speed, devices and coverage properly

889 + 662 + 564 messages in September alone

These three barely existed on the old flow and are now top-six topics. The education pages raise the question; the chat has to finish it. “स्पीड कितनी मिलती है?” is the single most typed free-text message in the whole dataset — people are typing it even when a chip exists.

Change: Give each a concrete, checkable answer — a number, a device count, a room count — not a reassurance.
03

Recharge is still the question that repeats

1,060 messages · 654 more asking what “recharge-wala ghar ka net” even means

Two separate chips exist for this and people still type it out. The concept — not the mechanics — is what is not landing.

Change: One worked example on the education pages: what you pay, when, and what happens if you don’t.

What this doesn’t tell us

No page views. We see messages, not visits, so there is no “what share of people on this page asked something”. A page’s message count reflects its traffic as much as its clarity.
structural
Silent chat opens are invisible. Only messages are recorded. Anyone who opened chat and typed nothing is not in any figure here.
structural
The rollout confounds the windows. August is mostly the old flow and September is mostly j2, so period comparisons carry a product change inside them. The old-vs-j2 section is the cleaner read.
design
The denominator differs from the June/July report. Warehouse first app-opens, not app-analytics installs. Internally consistent here; not comparable to that report.
definition
Pre-booking identity does not join to bookings. The id on these rows is a booking-service id, not a phone number, so we still cannot say whether the people who chatted went on to install.
structural
Sentiment is a model label, and only the customer side of the conversation is recorded. This measures what people said, not whether they were helped.

Glossary

Words used here

New user
A mobile whose first-ever app open falls in the window, from the warehouse app-opens table. 32,849 in 10–30 Aug, 62,743 in 1–20 Sep. Existing Wiom customers cannot appear in pre-booking chat at all.
Original pages
The six original booking pages: landing, how does it work, how to get started, net cost explanation, checkout, QR.
New screens
The new booking journey — education 1 and 2, cost, plans, slot selection, payment, user details — rolled out from mid-August. Recorded with a j2_ prefix, which is a screen-set name and is not the booking variant J2.
Chip
A suggested question. Detected as identical message text sent by three or more different people. This catches chips the classifier also processed as free text, which a simpler definition misses.
Dead end
A message the classifier resolved to FallbackIntent — the chat had no answer.
Frustrated
Share of messages the production model tagged FRUSTRATED or ANGRY.
